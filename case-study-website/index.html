Church Website Design: UX Design Case Study

Enhancing Parish Websites for Improved User Experience

📥 Download Full Case Study (PDF)

Download the executive presentation (PDF)

Project Overview

Product: Websites for The Church of the Most Precious Blood (Dover, MA) and Saint Theresa of Lisieux Parish (Sherborn, MA)
Duration: January 2011 to May 2011
Role: Lead UX Designer (Volunteer)
Responsibilities: Conducting interviews and usability studies, wireframing, prototyping, and ensuring responsive and accessible design

The Problem

The parishes were working in partnership, but their websites did not effectively convey this collaboration. Navigation was challenging, and the visual appeal was lacking. The websites also needed to meet the standards set by the Archdiocese of Boston.

The Goal

I redesigned the websites to highlight the partnership between the two parishes. I made them easy to use and visually appealing. I built the redesigned sites on Joomla, so staff could update them easily. The sites work on desktop, tablet, and phone, with clear navigation.

Understanding the User

I conducted user research through interviews and empathy maps to understand the target audience's needs. Insights showed the importance of convenience and accessibility in online platforms for community engagement. Poor navigation significantly impacted visitors' ability to locate information and connect with the church.

User Research: Pain Points

The pain points fell into three groups: navigation (confusing menus, poor search, and broken links), interaction (cumbersome forms, buggy interfaces, and lack of feedback), and experience (cluttered design, unclear calls to action, no trust signals, poor content, and slow loading times).

Persona: Eleanor Vance

This persona is based on user research for this case study.

  • Age: 62
  • Occupation: Retired Teacher
  • Goals: Easily find parish information (services, contacts, events) and donate online.
  • Frustrations: Difficult navigation, outdated content, cluttered design, poor mobile experience.

User Journey Map

Eleanor’s journey highlights her struggles with the old website and the need for a more streamlined and accessible experience.

Features and Benefits

  • Simplified Navigation: Clear menus and intuitive layout to help users find key information quickly.
  • Online Donation Integration: Streamlined process for donations, improving ease of use and trust.
  • Responsive Design: Optimized for various devices, ensuring a consistent and accessible experience.
  • Clear Calls to Action: Improved visual hierarchy to guide users through the site effectively.

Starting the Design

  • Sitemap: Created a clear information architecture to address navigation issues, covering service times, directions, what to expect as a visitor, events, news, contact, and giving.
  • Wireframes: Designed for both paper and digital formats to visualize the improvements.
  • Low-Fidelity Prototype: Developed a basic prototype to test the primary user flow.

Usability Study: Findings

The study was unmoderated and remote, in the United States, with five participants. Each session lasted 20 to 30 minutes.

  1. Navigation Confusion
    Finding: Participants struggled to locate key information.
    Fix: Clearer navigation and information architecture.
  2. Donation Process Difficulty
    Finding: The online donation process was confusing and lengthy, with unclear instructions.
    Fix: A shorter donation process with clear instructions, including a checkbox to copy the donation details into the contact fields.
  3. Mobile Responsiveness
    Finding: Layout issues appeared on smaller screens.
    Fix: A layout that adapts to smaller screens.

Refining the Design

  • Mockups: Revised based on usability study insights.
  • High-Fidelity Prototype: Finalized designs for detailed testing, incorporating team feedback and usability study findings.

Accessibility Considerations

  • Font Size and Contrast: Ensured sufficient font size and high contrast for readability
  • Keyboard Navigation: Designed the website to be navigable using a keyboard alone, with clear visual indicators of focus
  • Screen Reader Compatibility: Built the content and structure to support screen readers, using semantic HTML, alternative text for images, and proper heading structure

Takeaways

The redesign focused on navigation, the donation flow, and the mobile layout. The key lessons were the importance of user-centered design, iteration, and accessibility.

Next Steps

  1. Conduct follow-up usability testing on the redesigned websites.
  2. Identify additional areas of need and plan new features.
