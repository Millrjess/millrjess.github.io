My story

For most of my adult life, I worked out at a gym, tried to eat healthy, and made an effort to maintain a healthy weight. I understood the importance of exercise and nutrition, and I did my best to practice what I knew.

But after age 45, as I entered perimenopause and moved toward menopause, maintaining a healthy BMI and losing abdominal and visceral fat became much more difficult than it had been 25 years earlier. My body was changing. I have low estrogen levels and insulin resistance is another factor I am addressing. The strategies that seemed to work more easily when I was younger no longer felt as effective.

I want you to understand that weight management is not always as simple as eating less and exercising more. Aging, hormonal changes, insulin sensitivity, sleep, genetics, medications, and other metabolic factors can influence body weight and health. My story is not about abandoning healthy habits. It is about recognizing that sometimes we need additional tools and medical support as our bodies change.

I recently began a new chapter focused on metabolic health, sustainable weight management, and healthy aging. After years of putting everyone else first, I decided it was my turn. I am on Zepbound, and here is the honest truth. When you lose weight fast, your body does not just burn fat. It eats muscle too. After 50, muscle is everything. It is what keeps you strong, steady on your feet, and burning calories at rest. Losing it was not an option for me, so protein became my number one focus.

I picked Zepbound over the other GLP-1s because it works on two appetite hormones instead of one, and the research showed people lost more weight on it. That was my decision with my doctor, and I would tell anyone to have that conversation with theirs. Zepbound is a prescription medication that acts on GIP and GLP-1 receptors. It can help eligible patients lose weight and improve certain measures of metabolic health.

The medicine curbs my appetite, but it does not feed me. That is on me. I eat the things on this page because I am trying to make sure what I do eat is actually healthy and keeps my muscle where it belongs. I am not claiming it prevents heart attacks, strokes, or diabetes. Cardiovascular risk depends on many factors, and treatment decisions should be individualized.

My goals for healthy aging

I want to feel better, move more comfortably, maintain my independence, protect my muscle, and have the energy to enjoy life as I get older. Improved physical health, better sleep, more mobility, and greater confidence can contribute to feeling better emotionally. But I will never claim that weight loss or stable blood sugar automatically improves mental health or treats depression.

My message is that we deserve access to evidence-based tools, compassionate medical care, and realistic information about our changing bodies. Being over 45 does not mean giving up on our health. It means learning what works for us now, rather than judging ourselves by what worked 25 years ago.

What this brand stands for

My brand represents a realistic approach to weight management and healthy aging for women over 45. I spent years trying to do the right things. Now I am exploring additional evidence-based tools to support my health as my body changes.

I want to share what I learn about medication, nutrition, protein, collagen, fiber, exercise, and wellness products so other people can make informed decisions about their own journeys. The message is not that everyone needs medication or supplements. The message is that getting older should not mean giving up on feeling stronger, healthier, and more confident.

My turning point: why I changed my mind about GLP-1 medication

For a long time, I was cynical about taking a GLP-1 medication. My biggest concern was that once you stopped taking it, the weight would simply come back. I questioned whether it made sense to start a medication if maintaining the results meant taking it long term.

Then I learned something that changed my perspective: obesity is a chronic condition, and for many people, long-term treatment may be necessary to maintain weight loss.

I began to understand that these medications are not necessarily meant to be temporary fixes. For eligible patients, long-term weight management can involve continuing medication, much like cholesterol management may require ongoing statin treatment.

What really caught my attention was the possibility of continuing treatment after reaching a goal weight, with an individualized maintenance plan designed to help prevent weight regain. The appropriate maintenance dose depends on the specific medication, prescribing guidance, tolerability, and the patient’s medical needs. A smaller dose is not automatically appropriate for everyone.

When I understood that weight management could be a long-term medical journey rather than a short-term diet, my perspective changed. I was sold on exploring this option with my healthcare provider.

Why prevention matters to me

I have a family history of heart attacks and strokes, and I do not want to develop type 2 diabetes if I can reduce my risk.

As I get older, I want to take preventive medicine seriously. I want to address the health risks I can influence rather than wait until a preventable problem becomes a serious diagnosis.

For me, this journey is about much more than a number on the scale. It is about improving my metabolic health, managing my weight, supporting healthy blood sugar levels, preserving muscle, and working toward a healthier future.

Excess weight, particularly when accompanied by metabolic risk factors, can increase the risk of several chronic conditions. Maintaining a healthier weight can improve important health markers and may lower the risk of certain weight-related complications. However, no particular weight range guarantees freedom from illness, and not every health outcome is determined by body size.

What I want other women over 45 to know

I realized that I needed to consider more than willpower. I needed to understand my changing body and explore the medical options available to me.

I am sharing my journey because I believe preventive healthcare, evidence-based treatment, nutrition, strength training, and realistic long-term planning all have a place in healthy aging.

GLP-1-based medications are not right for everyone, and they are not magic. But for eligible people, they can be valuable medical tools. I want to share what I learn honestly, including the benefits, costs, challenges, and importance of long-term maintenance.

My goal is not simply to lose weight. My goal is to protect my health, preserve my independence, and give myself the best opportunity to enjoy the years ahead.

This is my personal journey, not medical advice. Talk to your own clinician about what is right for you.

💦 Water, water, water! Hydration matters! 💦

When you are taking a GLP-1 medication like Zepbound and eating less, it is especially important not to forget to drink enough water! 💧 Reduced food intake can also mean you are getting less fluid from food, so staying hydrated needs to be part of your daily routine.

💙 My daily goal is 80 oz of water!

I order eight 5-gallon jugs of purified water for delivery every other month, or as needed, to keep plenty of water on hand. My daughter also has fresh water readily available in our kitchen with our bottom-loading water dispenser.

💰 Money-saving tip: Compare competing water delivery services and negotiate! Ask whether a company will match or beat a competitor’s price. It never hurts to ask!

As I focus on my GLP-1 weight loss journey, kidney health, and long-term wellness, I am reminding myself that eating less is only part of the picture. We also need to prioritize hydration, adequate protein, and proper nutrition.

Small daily habits can make a big difference! 💦💪
