My story

For most of my adult life, I worked out, tried to eat well, and did my best to stay healthy. After 45, as perimenopause arrived, the strategies that used to work stopped working. My body was changing and I needed to change with it.

I started Zepbound with my doctor's guidance. When you lose weight fast, your body burns muscle too, and after 50, muscle is everything. So protein became my number one focus, along with strength training to protect what I have.

I share what I learn and what I actually use, so other women over 45 can make informed decisions about their own health. Being older should not mean giving up on feeling strong.

This is my personal journey, not medical advice. Talk to your clinician about what is right for you.

My turning point

I was skeptical about GLP-1 medication for a long time. What changed my mind was learning that obesity is a chronic condition, and for many people, long-term treatment is part of managing it, the way blood pressure medication works for hypertension.

Once I saw it as medical care rather than a shortcut, I talked to my doctor and started this chapter.

What I wish I knew about muscle loss on Zepbound

Monday is shot day for me. The scale is moving in the right direction, and I am grateful for that. But there is something nobody told me when I started, and it matters more than the number on the scale. Read moreShow less

When you lose weight fast, your body does not only burn fat. It breaks down muscle too. In the clinical trials, researchers measured this with DXA scans, and the numbers surprised me. In the STEP-1 trial, up to 40 percent of the weight lost was lean mass rather than fat. For Zepbound specifically, the SURMOUNT-1 trial showed about 25 percent. To be precise, the scans measure lean mass, which includes water and tissue along with muscle. The honest version is this: a meaningful share of rapid weight loss can be lean mass, not fat.

That hits harder in your fifties. Muscle is what keeps you strong, steady on your feet, and burning calories at rest. After 50, muscle is independence. Losing it quietly while the scale celebrates is the part nobody warned me about.

The good news is that two levers push back, and the research supports them together. A 2025 analysis in Nutrition Journal found that the muscle preserving benefit came from higher protein combined with resistance training, not from either one alone. Researchers studying older adults generally suggest around 1.0 to 1.2 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight per day during weight loss. My own target is 90 grams a day, which is the default I set in my tracker.

My biggest practical change was front loading protein at breakfast. Thirty grams at breakfast changes the whole day. You stay full, your muscle stays protected, and hitting your daily goal stops being a dinner time scramble. My move is a vanilla protein shake before coffee: Orgain vanilla bean blended with cold brew, ice, and a splash of milk. It tastes like a treat and gives me 21 grams before my day even starts.

The second lever is resistance training. I do strength work twice a week. Nothing extreme. Dumbbells, resistance bands, bodyweight exercises, and Pilates. I started with 10 pound weights and worked up from there. The goal is not to look like a bodybuilder. The goal is to keep the muscle I have.

A few habits that made this easier for me. Eat protein first at every meal, because on Zepbound it is easy to feel full before you have had enough of it. Rotate your injection sites: abdomen two inches from the navel, front of the thigh, or back of the upper arm, left and right. I log every shot with its site in my tracker so I never have to guess where last week went. And track the protein, the water, and the movement every day. What gets logged gets protected.

One honest note on collagen. I use Vital Proteins collagen peptides in my coffee or shakes, and I like it. A 2021 meta analysis of 19 trials found that collagen peptides modestly improved skin hydration and elasticity over about 90 days. That is real. But no study has shown that collagen prevents loose skin after substantial weight loss. For that, the realistic options are building muscle underneath, in office tightening procedures, or surgery. I would rather tell you the truth than sell you a miracle.

Five-Minute Makeup That Loves Mature Skin

I do not have time for a 12-step routine. Here is what actually works on my 52-year-old skin in five minutes. Step one: moisturizer. Always. Mature skin needs hydration before anything else goes on. I let it sink in for a minute while I pick out clothes. Read moreShow less

Step two: tinted moisturizer or light foundation. I stopped wearing heavy foundation years ago. It settles into every line. A sheer formula evens things out without looking like a mask.

Step three: cream blush on the apples of my cheeks. Powder blush can look dry on mature skin. Cream melts in and looks like you just came in from a walk.

Step four: mascara. One coat, top lashes only. It opens up your whole face.

Step five: tinted lip balm. Color and moisture in one step.

That is it. Five products, five minutes. I look like myself, just a little more awake.

My Morning Routine on Zepbound

Mornings set the tone for my whole day. Here is what mine looks like now. I wake up and drink a full glass of water before anything else. Hydration first, always. Then I make my protein coffee shake. Orgain vanilla bean, cold brew, ice, a splash of milk. I sip it while I get ready. Read moreShow less

I check in with my body. How am I feeling today? Any nausea? How is my energy? I jot a quick note in my tracker. It takes 30 seconds and it helps me see patterns over time.

If it is a strength training day, I do 20 minutes with dumbbells after my shake settles. If not, I take a walk. Movement does not have to be intense. It just has to happen.

Then I get dressed, do my five-minute makeup, and start my day. The whole routine takes about an hour, and it makes everything after it easier.

The biggest change: I stopped skipping breakfast. On Zepbound, it is easy to not feel hungry in the morning. But my body needs fuel, especially protein. The shake makes it simple.

Embracing My Silver: My Gray Hair Story

My mom went gray at 30 and never dyed her hair. Today, it is pure white, just as it was when she was a toddler. With her crystal blue eyes, her natural coloring is stunning. My mom was ahead of her time. She never chased a trend or covered what nature gave her. Read moreShow less

After years of babylights, highlights, and balayage, I am finally embracing my own silver. I stopped dyeing my hair because as we get older, hair naturally thins. I was highlighting and bleaching to camouflage incoming silver, but it ended up looking damaged. My hair felt dry and was even starting to fall out.

Going natural is not just a style choice. It is a simpler one. Skipping the salon cycle means my hair is not processed anymore, and it shows. It feels stronger and looks healthier than it has in years. I feel more confident not worrying about toner, the right shade of blonde, or whether it is too yellow or brassy. It becomes expensive and exhausting to maintain a perfect look that still feels fake.

What I use to keep my silver bright:

  • Aveda Blue Malva, a plant based shampoo that keeps brassiness away.
  • Oribe Silverati, made to brighten and soften natural gray.
  • SEEN, fragrance free and gentle on a sensitive scalp.
  • Acure and AG Care, budget friendly and sulfate free, with apple cider vinegar for shine.

My mom went gray at 30 and never looked back. I understand why now. Sometimes, the most beautiful thing you can do is be natural.
