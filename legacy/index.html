Jack Anthony Mauro

Optical engineer and inventor

My grandfather, Dr. Jack Mauro, worked in optics at General Electric in Pittsfield, Massachusetts. He was the inventor named on U.S. Patent 3,206,603, for an infrared flaw detector, assigned to General Electric, and he edited the General Electric Optical Engineering Handbook in 1963. He spent his life on light and sight.

The city is in the beautiful Berkshires of Western Massachusetts, where we get away several times a year. My parents retired there.

The family

The Mauro family is from Naples, in my family account. He was the son of Francis and Lucia (Buono) Mauro. He married Camille Dolores Montaperto. They had three children: Richard F., George E., and Barbara L. Richard is my father. They lived on Long Island, New York, and in Palm Beach, Florida.

What I remember

I grew up in New Mexico, close to him. When I was a young child, he would tell me stories of Ancient Greek philosophy, which is the foundation for all theoretical physics. I think he was a genius. The inventing runs in the family. I remember how his saxophone shone in the sun. He had a passion for music.

Why this matters to my work

The World Wide Web Consortium was founded at MIT in 1994. I built my earliest websites to its W3C standards, and I still work to those standards today.

My grandfather spent his working life with light and sight. My work is different, but I see a connection in my commitment to accessibility. That commitment is the standard I bring to my work and part of the legacy I see in him.
