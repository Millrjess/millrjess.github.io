Product

New Chapter: GLP-1 Tracker™ (Independent Product, Founder)

Problem: Most trackers log injections, protein, and movement in separate places. None I found was built around protecting strength as the central job.

A GLP-1 lifestyle tool with a personal AI coach. Everything in one place. Tracking data stays on device. Companion messages go to Cloudflare Workers AI with only a short summary of today and are not stored or used to train.

Founder · Designer and developer · Live on the web

Approach: I designed and built a progressive web app from scratch around a simple framework: eat enough protein, keep moving, never miss a shot.

A six-tab interface (Today, Shots, Weight, Kitchen, Companion, More) runs offline-first with a service worker and installs on iPhone and Android, using no frameworks and no build step. I integrated Stripe subscriptions at $3.99/month and $29.99/year, Google Analytics for usage insights, and a tab-delimited data export. Subscriptions and analytics sync when the device is online, while logging works offline.

The Companion is a real agentic AI, built on large language models on Cloudflare Workers AI. The Companion has opt-in memory for the user's name, goals, and preferences. Messages are rate-limited to 40 per user per day, origin-gated, with no API key in client code and guardrails against medical advice. I replaced a complex link-based onboarding with a simple six-digit access code.

Design decisions: I iterated the interface based on real user testing. I added Undo and per-entry Remove controls after testing revealed users could not fix mistakes. I ran a self-assessment against WCAG 2.2 AA, Apple Human Interface Guidelines, and W3C mobile guidance:

  • Text contrast ratios checked, with legible type sizes and 44px touch targets.
  • Keyboard and screen reader support with ARIA roles, plus visible focus indicators.
  • Reduced motion support, no orientation lock, and zoom not blocked.

I built an interactive injection site body map with keyboard navigation, ARIA labels, and state indicators that do not rely on color alone. I also built a custom design system around the brand's gold-and-sage palette. The layout is mobile-first and responsive.

Outcome: I designed, built, and launched it as an independent product, including a sales page and a custom app icon. The data export gives users a spreadsheet-ready record to share with their healthcare providers. Validation: usability testing with real users and a self-assessment against WCAG 2.2 AA. The app meets the same standards I apply to client sites. Sales page · Founder and product showcase (PDF).

Design case studies

Parish Website Redesign (Joomla)

Lead UX Designer · 2012 to 2013 · Volunteer

Problem: Two partner parishes, The Church of the Most Precious Blood and Saint Theresa of Lisieux, had websites that didn’t convey their collaboration. Navigation was confusing, search was poor, links were broken, pages loaded slowly, and neither site met Archdiocese of Boston standards.

What I learned from users: I ran user interviews and built empathy maps. The research showed that convenience and accessibility matter most for community engagement, and poor navigation kept people from finding information and connecting with the church. The primary persona was Eleanor Vance, a 62-year-old retired teacher whose goals were to find service times, contact info, and events, and to donate online.

Approach: I built a sitemap to fix the information architecture, created paper and digital wireframes, and built a low-fidelity prototype to test the primary user flow. Usability studies found three problems: key information was hard to locate, the donation process was confusing and lengthy, and the layout broke on smaller screens. I revised the mockups around those findings, simplifying the donation process and the navigation, and finalized a high-fidelity prototype. I built the redesign on Joomla so parish staff could update content themselves. Accessibility was included from the start: sufficient font size and contrast, full keyboard navigation, and screen reader compatibility.

Outcome: I delivered a redesigned, responsive, accessible site with simplified navigation, intuitive layout, and integrated online donations. See the case study, the case study PDF, and the executive presentation (PDF).

Foodie Explorer: End-to-End UX Case Study

Lead UX Designer · January to June 2025 · Capstone project

Problem: David, a busy professional, needs a reliable and efficient way to look up recipes and order groceries online. Time constraints are the top pain point in meal planning and grocery shopping. The app needs to remove friction, not add features.

What I learned from users: I ran user research through surveys and usability tests. The persona was David Miller, a 42-year-old freelance photographer who wants to save time, enjoy family time, explore new foods, support local businesses, and simplify life. He is frustrated by time constraints, grocery hassle, limited cooking time, difficulty finding quality ingredients, and inconsistent quality. The pain points are complex navigation in existing apps and limited tailored recipe recommendations.

Approach: I followed a full UX lifecycle covering user research, synthesis, personas, journey mapping, wireframing, prototyping, and iterative design rounds. The design centered on the following:

  • Users can discover recipes by ingredients, dietary needs, cuisine, or cooking time.
  • Recipes are quick and easy, ready in 30 minutes or less.
  • Each recipe includes step-by-step visual guides.
  • Grocery lists build themselves from the recipes you choose.
  • The design includes meal planning tools and community features for sharing recipes.

Usability testing confirmed that search was intuitive, visual guides raised user confidence, and navigation was streamlined. I refined the mockups for personalization and accessibility, with high-contrast mode and readable fonts, voice search and screen reader support, and clear navigation with large tap targets.

Outcome: I delivered a complete, research-backed mobile app design with a video walkthrough of every design decision. Case study video on YouTube · Full case study · Executive presentation (PDF).

AI Cybersecurity Agents: Concept Case Study

Concept case study · Built to order · Highest-priced service

Problem: Attackers now use AI agents to break into websites. In October 2026, researchers documented autonomous AI agents probing more than 1,200 company websites and reaching sites belonging to more than 100 companies, at about $5 per attempt, and about $50 per successful intrusion. At that price, a small business website is worth trying.

What this is: a concept case study, not a finished client project. No client agent has been delivered yet. The page shows the design and the build: four jobs (predict, intercept, block, clean up damage), defense in three layers (the playbook, care that keeps the site current, agents built to order), and a playbook that pre-authorizes exactly two actions. I oversee each agent myself, and nothing an agent does goes live without my check.

Approach: I start with the business problem, not the tool. I assess the website, write the playbook, build the agent from the playbook, and test it against the site and real attack patterns. The agent is designed to run on Cloudflare at the edge, with an origin-side component that scans files and restores clean copies from backup.

Outcome: an agent built to order for one website, from its playbook, tested before it goes live. Custom build from $7,500 per site, as an add-on to a care plan at $200 a month per agent. Full concept case study · Executive presentation (PDF).

Data and business intelligence

Predictive Analytics Dashboard Pipeline (SQL / Tableau)

Data science and visualization · Python, SQL, Tableau, BigQuery

Problem: Manual reporting was slow and error-prone, and decision makers needed a data-driven view of what drives protest activity in the US.

Approach: I combined ACLED event data with BLS economic indicators, built a negative binomial regression model to identify associations, and piped the results into an automated Tableau dashboard. The reporting now refreshes itself instead of requiring manual work each time. The dashboard presents the same model through three views: coefficients for policymakers, protest patterns by day of week and month for community organizers, and an accessible overview of trends and drivers for the general public. I created a dashboard view that separates location indicators from the selected socioeconomic variables, making those variables easier to inspect.

Outcome: The model is a negative binomial regression on 3,523 state-month observations (ACLED event counts with BLS unemployment, data through January 2026). Log population exposure carries a coefficient of 0.8017, unemployment 0.1131, and the time trend 0.0342. These are associations, not proof of cause. The automated pipeline keeps the reporting current. Notebook on GitHub · Dashboard on Tableau Public.
